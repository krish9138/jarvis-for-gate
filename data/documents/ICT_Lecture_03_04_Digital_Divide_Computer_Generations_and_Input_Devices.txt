## 1. Introduction to ICT & The Digital Divide
- **Definition of ICT Tools**: A diverse range of technological goods, products, and services used for digital processing, storage, and electronic communication of information.
- **The Digital Divide (4 Interrelated Pillars)**:
  1. **Awareness**: Lack of knowledge about technology's capability and potential.
  2. **Accessibility**: Usability constraints including digital literacy, language barriers, and accessible UI.
  3. **Availability**: Geographic proximity to physical hardware, telecommunication infrastructure, and connectivity.
  4. **Affordability**: Total cost of ownership (devices, software licenses, broadband subscriptions).
  *(Key Insight: The digital divide is not merely economic; it involves social, cultural, geographic, and political divides).*
- **Key Challenges in Developing Countries**:
  - High import duties on hardware and proprietary software licensing fees. (Solution: Open-source software like Linux, Blender).
  - Infrastructure fragility (frequent power failures, weak rural connectivity).
  - Internet governance imbalances (inequitable historical distribution of IPv4 address space and low representation in global standards bodies).

## 2. Evolution & Five Generations of Computers
| Generation | Period | Core Hardware Technology | Primary Memory / Storage | Software & Languages | Characteristics & Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st Gen** | 1940–1956 | **Vacuum Tubes** | Magnetic drums, paper tape | Machine Language (Binary 0s/1s) | Enormous size, very high heat, unreliable. *Examples: ENIAC, UNIVAC, IBM 7094.* |
| **2nd Gen** | 1956–1963 | **Transistors** | Magnetic core memory, magnetic tape | Assembly Language & Early HLL (FORTRAN, COBOL) | Smaller, faster, energy efficient. *Examples: IBM 1401, PDP-10.* |
| **3rd Gen** | 1964–1971 | **Integrated Circuits (ICs)** (SSI & MSI) | Magnetic disk storage | Structured High-Level Languages | Keyboard/monitor interfaces, operating systems. *Examples: IBM System/360, PDP-11.* |
| **4th Gen** | 1971–Present | **Microprocessors (VLSI / LSI)** | Semiconductor RAM, ROM, HDDs, SSDs | Modern OOP Languages, GUIs | Personal computing explosion (PCs, laptops, smartphones). *Examples: Intel 4004/8086, Apple Mac.* |
| **5th Gen** | Present–Future | **ULSI (Ultra Large-Scale Integration) & AI** | Cloud storage, high-density flash, quantum | Natural language processing, parallel AI | Artificial Intelligence, neural networks, robotics. *Examples: PARAM supercomputers, Fugaku.* |

## 3. Computer Classifications & Supercomputing Power (FLOPS)
- **Computer Hierarchy by Size & Power**:
  1. **Personal Computers / Laptops**: Single-user, general-purpose tasks.
  2. **Workstations**: High-performance single-user systems for engineering CAD, software development, 3D rendering.
  3. **Minicomputers (Midrange)**: Multi-user systems (tens to hundreds) for departmental processing.
  4. **Mainframes**: Ultra-reliable high-throughput multi-user machines (thousands) optimized for **massive volumes of concurrent transactions** (banks, airlines).
  5. **Supercomputers**: Ultra-high-speed systems optimized for **complex scientific parallel calculations** (weather modeling, aerodynamics, molecular simulation).
- **FLOPS (Floating Point Operations Per Second)**:
  - Standard metric for supercomputer calculation performance (unlike Clock Speed in GHz for normal CPUs).
  - **MegaFLOPS ($10^6$) $\rightarrow$ GigaFLOPS ($10^9$) $\rightarrow$ TeraFLOPS ($10^{12}$) $\rightarrow$ PetaFLOPS ($10^{15}$) $\rightarrow$ ExaFLOPS ($10^{18}$)**.
  - *Indian Benchmark*: **Pratyush** & **Mihir** (HPC systems operating in PetaFLOPS for meteorological forecasting).

## 4. Fundamental Computer Architecture: Input Devices & Interfaces
### A. The IPO Cycle (Input-Processing-Output):
- Data flows: Input Unit $\rightarrow$ RAM / Primary Memory $\leftrightarrow$ CPU (ALU + Control Unit + Registers) $\rightarrow$ Output Unit / Secondary Storage.
- **GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out)**: Highlights that a computer possesses Zero IQ and blindly executes instructions.

### B. Specialized Input Devices:
1. **Pointing Devices**:
   - **Mouse & Touchpad**: 2D coordinate displacement sensors.
   - **Trackball**: Stationary device with an exposed rotating ball (ergonomic).
   - **Joystick**: Potentiometer-based lever for flight simulation and gaming.
2. **Graphic & Optical Input**:
   - **Graphics Tablet / Digitizer**: Pressure-sensitive surface with electromagnetic stylus for digital drawing and CAD.
   - **Light Pen**: Light-sensitive wand detecting CRT electron beam raster scan (legacy).
   - **Scanner & OCR (Optical Character Recognition)**: Converts physical documents to raster images; OCR software parses raster text into editable ASCII/Unicode characters.
   - **OMR (Optical Mark Reader)**: Senses presence or absence of dark pencil/pen marks via light reflectivity on objective exam sheets.
   - **MICR (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition)**: Reads bank cheque numbers printed with magnetic iron-oxide ink (E-13B font) via magnetic field sensing (highly secure against forgery).
   - **Barcode vs QR Code**:
     - *1D Barcode*: 1-dimensional vertical lines/spaces of varying widths.
     - *2D QR Code (Quick Response)*: 2D matrix storing vastly more data; omnidirectional readability with built-in Reed-Solomon error correction.
3. **Touchscreens**:
   - **Resistive**: Pressure-sensitive dual-layer; operated with finger or stylus.
   - **Capacitive**: Senses electrostatic field of human touch; highly responsive multi-touch (standard in smartphones).
4. **Audio & Sensor Input**:
   - **Microphone**: Transducer converting sound waves into analog electrical voltages $\rightarrow$ digitized via Sound Card ADC.
   - **MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface)**: Technical communication protocol; transmits digital music instruction events (note on/off, pitch, velocity), NOT raw audio waveforms.
