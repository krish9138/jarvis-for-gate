## 1. Development of Surfaces: Special Solids & Transition Pieces
- **Transition Pieces**: Ducts/fittings connecting two conduits of different shapes or cross-sections (e.g., square-to-round, rectangular-to-circular).
- **Triangulation Method**: Specifically used to develop the lateral surface of transition pieces by dividing the non-developable surface into a series of contiguous triangles.
- **Development of Doubly Curved Surfaces (Spheres)**:
  - Exact development of a sphere is mathematically impossible (doubly curved non-developable surface).
  - **Approximation Methods**:
    1. **Zone Method**: Sphere is divided into horizontal zones/strips (frustums of cones).
    2. **Lune / Gore Method**: Sphere is divided into vertical lunes/gores (sections resembling orange slices).

### Lateral Surface Development Summary:
| Solid Shape | Development Method | Resulting 2D Flat Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Prism** (e.g., square, hexagonal) | Parallel-line development | Rectangle composed of contiguous rectangular faces |
| **Cylinder** (right circular) | Parallel-line development | Rectangle of width = $\pi D$ and height = $H$ |
| **Pyramid** | Radial-line development | Triangular segments sharing a common apex |
| **Cone** (right circular) | Radial-line development | Sector of a circle of radius = slant height $L$, sector angle $\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{L}$ |
| **Transition Piece** | Triangulation development | Series of contiguous triangles |
| **Sphere** | Approximation (Zone / Lune) | Circular arcs (Zone) or curved gores (Lune) |

### Key Mathematical Relations in Cone Development:
- Subtended angle of sector: $\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{L} = 180^\circ \times \frac{d}{L}$
- **Special Case**: When development of cone is a **semicircle** ($\theta = 180^\circ$):
  $$180^\circ = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{L} \implies L = 2r = d$$
  *(Slant height equals the base diameter; in front view, the cone appears as an **equilateral triangle**).*

## 2. Intersection of Surfaces (Interpenetration of Solids)
- **Purpose**: When two solids intersect, their common junction forms a **Line or Curve of Intersection**. Determining this curve is vital in sheet-metal fabrication (funnels, chimneys, boiler shells, pipelines, ducts) to ensure the joint is **strongest and leak-proof**.

### Methods of Determining Curve of Intersection:
1. **Line or Generator Method**: Uses imaginary lines/generators on the surface of one solid to find piercing points on the other.
2. **Cutting Plane Method**: Series of parallel horizontal or vertical cutting planes slicing both solids simultaneously to yield overlapping lines/circles whose intersections form points on the curve.

### Nature of Intersection (Line vs Curve):
| Interpenetrating Solids | Resulting Intersection Profile |
|---|---|
| **Prism to Prism** (plane to plane) | Set of linear / straight line segments |
| **Prism to Cylinder** (plane to curved) | Curved profile / trace |
| **Prism to Pyramid** (plane to plane) | Straight intersecting lines |
| **Prism to Cone** (plane to curved) | Curved profile |
| **Cylinder to Cylinder** (curved to curved) | Curved profile |
| **Cone to Cone** | Curved profile |
| **Two Overlapping Spheres** | Exact **Circular boundary** |
| **Tapered Structure (Cone) cut by a flat plane** | **Conical section** (Circle, Ellipse, Parabola, Hyperbola) |
| **Vertical Cylinder cut by vertical secondary plane** | **Straight vertical edge** |
| **Vertical Prism cut by horizontal plane** | **Straight boundary** |

## 3. Practice Questions & Numerical Calculations
1. **Development of Lateral Surface of a Rectangle (120 mm × 60 mm)**:
   - Square prism: $4 \times 30 = 120\text{ mm}$ (Valid)
   - Hexagonal prism: $6 \times 20 = 120\text{ mm}$ (Valid)
   - Cylinder: $\pi D = \pi \times (120/\pi) = 120\text{ mm}$ (Valid)
   - $\rightarrow$ **All of the above are correct.**
2. **Shortest String Around a Cone**:
   - Shortest path starting from a point on the base, wound around the cone, and returning to the same point equals the **largest chord of the development sector**.
3. **Development of Tetrahedron (60 mm edge)**:
   - Consists of 4 equilateral triangles of 60 mm side $\rightarrow$ represented as a single large **equilateral triangle of side 120 mm**.
4. **Shortest String Around Hexagonal Prism (20 mm base side, 50 mm axis)**:
   - Total perimeter $W = 6 \times 20 = 120\text{ mm}$, height $H = 50\text{ mm}$.
   - Shortest path = hypotenuse: $\sqrt{120^2 + 50^2} = \sqrt{14400 + 2500} = \sqrt{16900} = 130\text{ mm}$.
