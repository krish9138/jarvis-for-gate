## 1. Classification of Solids
A solid is a 3-dimensional object having length, breadth, and height/thickness.

### A. Polyhedra (Solids bounded by plane faces)
1. **Regular Polyhedra (Platonic Solids - all faces identical regular polygons)**:
   - **Tetrahedron**: 4 equal equilateral triangular faces.
   - **Cube / Hexahedron**: 6 equal square faces.
   - **Octahedron**: 8 equal equilateral triangular faces.
   - **Dodecahedron**: 12 equal regular pentagonal faces.
   - **Icosahedron**: 20 equal equilateral triangular faces.
2. **Prisms**:
   - Polyhedron having two identical, parallel end faces (bases) and rectangular/parallelogram lateral faces.
   - Named by base shape: Triangular, Square, Pentagonal, Hexagonal, Octagonal prisms.
3. **Pyramids**:
   - Polyhedron having a plane polygon base and a number of triangular lateral faces meeting at a common point called the **Apex / Vertex**.
   - Named by base shape: Triangular, Square, Pentagonal, Hexagonal pyramids.

### B. Solids of Revolution (Generated by revolving a plane 2D figure about an axis)
1. **Cylinder**: Generated by revolving a **Rectangle** about one of its sides.
2. **Cone**: Generated by revolving a **Right-angled Triangle** about one of its perpendicular sides (hypotenuse forms generators).
3. **Sphere**: Generated by revolving a **Semicircle** about its diameter.

### C. Right Solid vs Oblique Solid
- **Right Solid**: Axis is strictly **perpendicular** to the base.
- **Oblique Solid**: Axis is **inclined** to the base.

### D. Frustum vs Truncated Solid
- **Frustum**: Obtained when a pyramid or cone is cut by a cutting plane **parallel to its base** and the apex portion is removed. Both top and bottom surfaces are parallel similar polygons/circles.
  - *Example*: Top view of frustum of cone on HP consists of **two concentric circles**.
- **Truncated Solid**: Obtained when a solid (prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone) is cut by a cutting plane **inclined to its base** and the upper portion is removed.

## 2. Standard Axis Orientation Conditions (Projections)
- **Golden Rule**: Always start by drawing the view that shows the **True Shape of the Base** first!
1. **Condition 1: Axis Perpendicular to H.P. & Parallel to V.P.**:
   - Top View shows True Shape of Base. Front View shows rectangular (prism/cylinder) or triangular (pyramid/cone) outline with vertical axis. (Drawn in **1 Stage**).
2. **Condition 2: Axis Perpendicular to V.P. & Parallel to H.P.**:
   - Front View shows True Shape of Base. Top View shows length/height. (Drawn in **1 Stage**).
3. **Condition 3: Axis Inclined to H.P. & Parallel to V.P.**:
   - Drawn in **2 Stages**:
     - *Stage 1*: Assume axis $\perp$ to HP $\rightarrow$ draw true shape base in TV, elevation in FV.
     - *Stage 2*: Tilt FV by required angle to XY $\rightarrow$ project final TV.
4. **Condition 4: Axis Inclined to V.P. & Parallel to H.P.**:
   - Drawn in **2 Stages**:
     - *Stage 1*: Assume axis $\perp$ to VP $\rightarrow$ draw true shape base in FV, plan in TV.
     - *Stage 2*: Tilt TV by required angle to XY $\rightarrow$ project final FV.
5. **Condition 5: Axis Inclined to BOTH H.P. and V.P.**:
   - Drawn in **3 Stages**.
- **Impossible Position**: Axis of a solid **CANNOT be perpendicular to both HP and VP simultaneously** (since HP and VP are mutually perpendicular).

## 3. Special Cases of Resting Solids (Lecture 19)
### Case 1: Pyramid Resting on One of its Triangular (Slant) Faces on H.P.
- The slant triangular face lies entirely on the ground (HP) $\rightarrow$ in Front View, the slant generator line lies on the reference line XY.
- Drawn in 2 stages: Simple position with base on HP $\rightarrow$ tilt FV until slant face lies on XY.

### Case 2: Pyramid Resting on One of its Slant Edges on H.P.
- The true length of the slant edge lies on XY in Front View.

### Case 3: Cone Resting on One of its Generators on H.P.
- In Front View, one extreme generator lies on XY. Axis is inclined at semi-apex angle $\alpha$ to the ground.

## 4. Lectures 18 & 19 - Practice Questions & Answer Key
1. **Q1**: Axis perpendicular to bases $\rightarrow$ **(B) Right solid**.
2. **Q2**: Which has equal faces $\rightarrow$ **(D) All of the above** (Tetrahedron, Cube, Octahedron are regular polyhedra).
3. **Q3**: Example of solids of revolution $\rightarrow$ **(D) All of the above** (Sphere, Cone, Cylinder).
4. **Q4**: Solid cut by plane parallel to base $\rightarrow$ **(C) Frustum of solid**.
5. **Q5**: Solid cut by plane inclined to base $\rightarrow$ **(B) Truncated solid**.
6. **Q6**: Kind of polyhedron having two parallel identical faces $\rightarrow$ **(B) Prism**.
7. **Q7**: Sphere generated by revolution of $\rightarrow$ **(C) Semicircle**.
8. **Q8**: Position NOT possible in solids $\rightarrow$ **(D) Axis of a solid perpendicular to both HP and VP**.
9. **Q9**: Frustum of cone on HP base, top view consists of $\rightarrow$ **(C) Two circles (concentric)**.
10. **Q10**: Front views of 1st, 2nd, and final stages of square prism with axis inclined to HP and base edge inclined to VP $\rightarrow$ **(A) Rectangle, rectangle, hexagon**.
11. **Q11**: Square pyramid with axis inclined at $45^\circ$ to HP and parallel to VP stages required $\rightarrow$ **(B) Two stages**.
