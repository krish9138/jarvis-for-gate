## 1. Section of Solids: Fundamental Concepts & Purpose
- **Why Draw Sectional Views?**:
  1. To clearly reveal the **internal details, cavities, holes, and assembly relationships** of complex mechanical components.
  2. To avoid **excessive, confusing dashed (hidden) lines** on exterior views.

### Types of Sectional Views:
1. **Full Section**: The cutting plane passes entirely through the solid, cutting it into two halves (one half imagined discarded).
2. **Half Section**: Used for **symmetrical objects** to show both internal and external features in a single view. The cutting plane cuts one quarter ($1/4$) of the object; one fourth is discarded, leaving half the view sectioned and the other half unsectioned.
3. **Broken-out / Partial Section**: Localized interior view without cutting the full part.
4. **Revolved / Removed Section**: Section of a bar, spoke, or arm turned $90^\circ$ into the drawing plane.

---

## 2. Hatching / Section Lines Standards (ISO & BIS)
- Drawn in the precise areas where the cutting plane actually slices through solid material.
- **Line Specifications**: Continuous thin lines drawn uniformly at an angle of **$45^\circ$ to the horizontal** (or principal centerline).
- **Spacing**: Uniformly spaced approx. **2 mm to 3 mm apart**.
- Section lines on adjacent assembled parts are drawn in **opposite directions** (e.g., $+45^\circ$ and $-45^\circ$).

---

## 3. Special Sectioning Rules (Components Left Unsectioned)
### Rule 1: Thin Ribs and Webs
- If a rib/web is cut **longitudinally** (i.e. cutting plane parallel to the flat face / dividing its thickness) $\rightarrow$ **Section lines are NOT shown in the rib area** (left unsectioned).
- If a rib/web is cut **transversely / perpendicularly** to its thickness $\rightarrow$ **Section lines ARE drawn** in the cut area.

### Rule 2: Fasteners & Standard Machine Parts
- Machine elements such as **bolts, nuts, studs, shafts, spindles, cotters, keys, rivets, pins, and gear teeth** are **LEFT UNSECTIONED** when the cutting plane passes longitudinally along their axes.
- If cut perpendicular (cross-section) to their axis, section hatching is shown.

## 4. Cutting Plane Orientations & Section Shapes
1. **Cutting Plane Perpendicular to V.P. & Parallel to H.P.**:
   - Shown as a horizontal line in Front View.
   - **Sectional Top View** shows the **True Shape of the section**.
2. **Cutting Plane Perpendicular to H.P. & Parallel to V.P.**:
   - Shown as a line in Top View.
   - **Sectional Front View** shows the **True Shape of the section**.
3. **Auxiliary Inclined Plane (A.I.P.) (Inclined to H.P. & Perpendicular to V.P.)**:
   - Appears as an inclined line in Front View.
   - Sectional Top View is an apparent / foreshortened shape.
   - **True Shape** is obtained on an auxiliary plane set **Parallel to the cutting plane**.
4. **Auxiliary Vertical Plane (A.V.P.) (Inclined to V.P. & Perpendicular to H.P.)**:
   - Appears as an inclined line in Top View.
   - True shape obtained on an auxiliary plane parallel to the cutting plane.

### True Section Shapes of Common Solids:
- **Cube** cut by AIP passing through solid diagonal $\rightarrow$ **Rhombus** (or regular hexagon if passing through midpoints of 6 edges).
- **Square Pyramid** cut by AIP parallel to base edge $\rightarrow$ **Trapezium**.
- **Cone** cut by plane parallel to axis / profile plane $\rightarrow$ **Hyperbola**.
- **Pentagonal Prism** lying on rectangular face on HP cut by AVP for maximum edges $\rightarrow$ **Seven edges (Heptagon)** (5 lateral faces + 2 base ends).

## 5. Development of Surfaces Basics (Lecture 21)
- **Definition**: Unfolding the entire 3D exterior boundary surface of a solid onto a single 2D plane.
- **Lateral Surface**: Total outer surface of the solid *excluding* the top and bottom end bases.
- **Methods**:
  - **Parallel-Line Method**: Used for **Prisms and Cylinders**.
  - **Radial-Line Method**: Used for **Pyramids and Cones**.
  - **Triangulation Method**: Used for **Transition Pieces**.
  - **Zone / Lune Method**: Used for **Spheres (doubly curved surfaces)**.
- **Shortest Path on Solids**: An insect moving around the curved surface of a cone from a base point back to the starting point travels along the **straight line / chord connecting the boundary points on the 2D development sector**.
