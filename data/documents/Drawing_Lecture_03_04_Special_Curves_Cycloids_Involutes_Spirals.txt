## 1. Classification of Special Plane Curves
Special engineering curves are classified into three major families:
1. **Cycloidal Curves** (Cycloid, Epicycloid, Hypocycloid, Trochoids, Cardioid)
2. **Involute Curves** (Involute of Circle, Involute of Polygons)
3. **Spiral Curves** (Archimedean Spiral, Logarithmic Spiral)

---

## 2. Cycloidal Curves Family
### A. Cycloid:
- **Definition**: Curve generated by a point on the **circumference of a circle (generating circle, dia $D$)** that rolls without slipping along a **fixed straight line** for one complete revolution.
- **Base Line Length**: Equal to circumference of the generating circle $= \pi D = 2\pi r$.
- **Trochoids (Point not on circumference)**:
  - **Inferior Trochoid**: Generating point is **INSIDE** the generating circle ($r' < r$).
  - **Superior Trochoid**: Generating point is **OUTSIDE** the generating circle ($r' > r$).

### B. Epicycloid & Cardioid:
- **Epicycloid**: Curve traced by a point on the circumference of a generating circle of radius $r$ rolling without slipping on the **OUTSIDE of another directing circle of radius $R$**.
  - Subtended angle of directing arc: $\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{R}$.
- **Cardioid (Heart-Shaped Special Case)**:
  - When the diameter of the rolling circle equals the diameter of the directing circle ($r = R$):
  $$\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{r} = 360^\circ$$
  - Traces a complete **Cardioid**.

### C. Hypocycloid & Straight Line (Tusi Couple):
- **Hypocycloid**: Curve traced by a point on the circumference of a generating circle of radius $r$ rolling without slipping along the **INSIDE of a directing circle of radius $R$**.
  - Subtended angle: $\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{R}$.
- **Special Case of Hypocycloid (Straight Line Theorem)**:
  - When the diameter of directing circle is **TWICE** the diameter of generating circle ($R = 2r$):
  $$\theta = 360^\circ \times \frac{r}{2r} = 180^\circ$$
  - The hypocycloid degenerates into a **straight line** (the diameter of the directing circle).

## 3. Involute Curves Family
- **Definition**: Curve traced out by the free end of a perfectly flexible, inelastic thread/string as it is **unwound tautly from a cylinder, circle, or regular polygon**.
- **Involute of a Circle**:
  - The unwound line length $= \pi D$.
  - *Engineering Application*: **Involute gear tooth profiles** (maintains constant velocity ratio, minimizes friction and wear).
- **Involute of a Polygon**:
  - Unwound from equilateral triangle (3 arcs), square (4 arcs), pentagon (5 arcs), hexagon (6 arcs).
- **Tangent & Normal to Involute**:
  - The tangent to the base circle at the point of unwinding forms the **Normal to the Involute** at that instantaneous point.

## 4. Spiral Curves Family
### A. Archimedean Spiral:
- **Definition**: Curve traced out by a point moving with **uniform linear velocity along a straight line**, while the line itself rotates in a plane about one of its ends (the **Pole**) with **uniform angular velocity**.
- **Key Property**: The difference in consecutive radius vectors is strictly **CONSTANT**:
  $$r_1 - r_2 = r_2 - r_3 = \dots = \text{constant}$$
- **Terminology**:
  - **Pole ($O$)**: The fixed center point of rotation.
  - **Radius Vector**: Line joining any point on the curve with the pole.
  - **Vectorial Angle**: Angle between initial baseline and radius vector.
  - **Convolution**: One complete $360^\circ$ rotation of the generating line.
- *Number of divisions on baseline* $n = \text{Convolution} \times \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Vector Angle}}$.

### B. Logarithmic (Equiangular) Spiral:
- **Definition**: Curve traced by a point moving along a rotating line such that for equal angular increments, the **ratio of consecutive radius vectors is CONSTANT**:
  $$\frac{r_2}{r_1} = \frac{r_3}{r_2} = \dots = \text{constant}$$
- **Mathematical Progression**:
  - Vectorial angles increase in **Arithmetic Progression (AP)**.
  - Corresponding radius vectors increase in **Geometric Progression (GP)**.
